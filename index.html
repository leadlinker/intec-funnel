The Problem

Aerospace composite qualification is unforgiving. One missed step and the program slips a quarter.

The difference between a clean qualification campaign and a six-figure rework comes down to what you verify before the first specimen is cut. Most teams learn this the expensive way.

01

Wrong Lab, Wrong Accreditations

You hand off to a lab that holds ISO 9001 but not ISO 17025 or NADCAP AC7122. Six weeks in you discover the data will not pass prime review. Every specimen ran, zero defensible data.

02

Specimen Prep Drift

Machining tolerances, bonding, environmental preconditioning, instrumentation. Small deltas between labs invalidate comparison against your baseline. The program schedule assumes data you cannot use.

03

Data Delivery Surprise

Testing finishes on time but the raw data arrives in a format your prime rejects on review. Now you are in rework mode with a launch date that is already public internally.

The 12 checks aerospace engineers run before opening an RFQ

A plain-language checklist you can take into any qualification conversation. Built from what Intec sees every week across OEM, prime, and Tier 1 programs.

  1. Accreditation Stack VerificationConfirm the lab holds AS9100, ISO 17025, and the specific NADCAP codes your program requires (AC7118, AC7122, AC7130, AC7114).
  2. Specimen Prep ProtocolMachining tolerance, bonding procedure, and fixture standardization across all specimens in the campaign.
  3. Environmental PreconditioningHumidity, thermal cycling, and UV exposure protocols matched to end-use environment.
  4. Load Capacity EnvelopeThe static and fatigue range you actually need (5 lbf to 600,000 lbf capability matters for structural programs).
  5. Thermal Testing RangeHigh-temperature composite parts require non-ambient capability. Verify cure temp ceiling (850F and above for BMI and polyimide).
  6. NDT Method CoverageUltrasonic, impacting, and measurement/inspection protocols must match what your prime will accept.
  7. Data Delivery FormatRaw data, processed data, and report format. Confirm compatibility with your prime's review tooling before test one.
  8. Chain of Custody ProcedureHow specimens are received, tracked, stored, and returned. Critical for audit-traceable programs.
  9. Program Management ScaleMulti-year, multi-thousand-specimen campaigns need dedicated PM capacity. Ask explicitly.
  10. Manufacturing-Under-Roof OptionSame lab that qualifies your parts can build them. Removes transfer risk and accelerates to production.
  11. Materials BreadthEpoxies, BMI, polycyanates, polyimides, thermoplastics, metal matrix. Your supplier should handle your whole roadmap.
  12. Capacity and Lead Time Reality CheckPublished capability and actual queued capacity are different. Always verify.
